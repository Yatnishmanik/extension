# 📚 @brainstorming

## 📌 Overview
- **Trigger**: `@brainstorming`
- **Category**: For Students (Study, Writing & Office Productivity)
- **Purpose**: Brainstorm essays, research projects, product designs, or coding projects before jumpstarting the execution.
- **Best For**: Resolving writer's block, exploring creative concepts, and outlining early project scopes.

---

## 🚀 How to Trigger
In your Promptimity AI chat, type:
```text
@brainstorming [describe your raw, early ideas, goals, or the topic you need to explore]
```

---

## 💡 Key Capabilities & Features

- **Structured Concept Expansion**: Turn simple keywords or vague intents into detailed lists of ideas, themes, and design approaches.
- **Creative Writing Prompts**: Proposes outline angles, character arcs, argumentative theses, and conceptual frameworks for essays or reports.
- **Feasibility Analysis**: Reviews raw project ideas and assesses technical complexity, potential risks, and resource requirements.
- **Interactive Questioning**: Uses a structured dialogue format (often asking questions one at a time) to refine your vision without overwhelming you.
- **Mind Map & Structure Generation**: Organizes thoughts into neat, structured lists, bullet points, or markdown maps that you can immediately export.

---

## 🛠️ Real-world Examples

### Example 1: Essay Outline Brainstorming
**Prompt:**
> `@brainstorming I want to write an argumentative essay on how remote work impacts urban economics. Let's brainstorm 3 distinct thesis angles and key research points we could cover.`

### Example 2: New App Idea
**Prompt:**
> `@brainstorming I have an idea for a mobile app that helps students barter textbooks locally. Let's brainstorm key user features, potential security concerns, and ideas for the user interface.`

---

## 📘 Best Practices
1. **Slow Down First**: Focus entirely on exploration and ideas before committing to code or writing paragraphs.
2. **Be Open to Alternatives**: Explore multiple diverse approaches (e.g. conservative, moderate, aggressive) before picking one path.
3. **Capture All Assumptions**: Document all structural assumptions early on to avoid confusion later in the project.
